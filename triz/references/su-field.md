# Su-field (substance-field) analysis and standard solutions

Use this when the problem is about an **interaction**: a needed effect is missing or too weak,
an interaction is harmful, or something is hard to detect or measure.

## The model

A minimal working system ("su-field", вепль) has three elements:
- **S2**: the tool (the thing that acts);
- **S1**: the object (the thing acted upon, the product);
- **F**: the field or energy through which S2 acts on S1 (mechanical, thermal, chemical,
  electrical, magnetic, optical, acoustic; by analogy in software and business: a signal,
  an incentive, information, a rule, money).

Draw the problem as S1, S2, F and the link between them, and classify it:
1. **Incomplete**: an element is missing (no tool, no field) → complete the model.
2. **Insufficient**: all elements exist, the effect is too weak → strengthen or develop the model.
3. **Harmful**: the interaction causes damage → break or neutralize the harmful link.
4. **Measurement/detection problem** → build a measuring model.

## The 76 standard solutions, condensed

The full system has 76 standards in 5 classes. Below are the key moves of each class; they
cover most practical uses. Use the moves as prompts, then make them concrete.

### Class 1. Building and destroying su-fields (13 standards)
- **Complete an incomplete model**: add the missing tool or field.
- If you cannot change S1 or S2, **add a substance** inside them (internal complex model) or
  outside (external complex model), or use the **environment** as the added substance.
- When a precise, small effect is needed but the field is hard to control, apply a **maximal
  effect and remove the excess** (overfill, then correct; principle 16).
- To act on only part of an object, add a **protective substance** where the action is unwanted.
- **Destroy a harmful link** by introducing a third substance between S1 and S2 (possibly a
  modification of S1 or S2 itself), by drawing the harm onto a sacrificial element, or by
  introducing a second field that neutralizes the harmful one.

### Class 2. Developing su-fields (23 standards)
- **Chain** models (S2 acts on S3, which acts on S1) or **double** models (two fields on one object).
- Make the field more **controllable**: mechanical → thermal → chemical → electric → magnetic
  (by analogy: manual → rule-based → automated → adaptive).
- **Fragment** the tool (solid → segments → powder → liquid → gas → field).
- Increase **dynamism**, structure the field (use a structured, non-uniform field), structure the substance.
- **Coordinate rhythms**: match the frequency of the field to the natural frequency of the object
  (or deliberately mismatch it to avoid harm).
- **Ferro-fields**: add magnetic particles and a magnetic field for control (by analogy: add
  tagged, easily steered elements to an otherwise uncontrollable flow).

### Class 3. Transition to supersystem and micro level (6 standards)
- Combine the system with another one into a **bi- or poly-system** (identical, shifted
  properties, or opposite properties); then integrate the combined system back into one.
- **Transition to the micro level**: replace macro parts with substances or particles that do
  the job through their own properties.

### Class 4. Detection and measurement (17 standards)
- **Avoid measuring**: change the system so measurement becomes unnecessary.
- **Measure a copy** or an image instead of the object; replace measuring with two successive
  detections of change.
- **Add a marker** (a substance or signal that is easy to detect) inside or near the object, or use
  the environment as a marker.
- **Use natural effects and resonance**: measure a change in natural frequency, phase transitions,
  or a field that the object already generates.
- Move from measuring to **first and second derivatives** (detect change and rate of change).

### Class 5. Applying standards with minimal additions (17 standards)
- Introduce substances **without adding substances**: use voids, the environment, the
  decomposition or combination of existing substances, an added substance that disappears afterwards.
- Introduce fields **using fields already present**, especially from the environment.
- Use **phase transitions** and states that change with conditions.
- Use **natural physical, chemical and geometric effects**.
- Obtain needed particles by **decomposing** or **combining** substances already present.

## Mapping hints for non-physical systems
- S1: the product, user, data, request, customer.
- S2: the component, service, team, process step, tool.
- F: data, signals, incentives, rules, attention, money, time.
- Harmful link example: notifications (F) from the app (S2) annoy the user (S1). Insert a third
  substance (a digest), modify F (only on meaningful changes: condition), or let S1 control F
  (self-service, feedback).
