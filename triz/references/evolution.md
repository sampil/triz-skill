# Trends of technical system evolution

Systems evolve along recognizable lines. Use the trends to forecast the next generation of a
product or technology, to generate ideas ("what if we move one step further along this line?"),
or to check whether an idea goes with the direction of evolution (ideas that go against it
rarely last).

## S-curve
Every system goes through birth (slow progress, many problems), growth (rapid improvement,
investment), maturity (diminishing returns, optimization, cost cutting) and decline (replacement
by a new system on a new curve).
- Signs of maturity: improvements are small and expensive; competition on price and cosmetics;
  many patents of low level; the system becomes a component of a bigger system.
- Strategy: in birth, fix the bottleneck that blocks use; in growth, scale and specialize; in
  maturity, trim cost and look for the next S-curve; never over-optimize a declining system.

## The main trends

1. **Increasing ideality.** More useful functions, less cost, harm and complexity. The limit is
   a function performed without a dedicated system.
2. **Uneven development of parts.** Parts improve at different rates; the lagging part becomes
   the contradiction that limits the whole. Look for the lagging part.
3. **Increasing dynamism and controllability.** Rigid → one joint → many joints → flexible →
   liquid/gas → field. Fixed → adjustable → adaptive → self-adjusting.
   Software: hardcoded → configured → adaptive → self-tuning.
4. **Increasing complexity, then simplification (trimming).** Systems first add functions
   and parts, then integrate and trim them while keeping the functions.
5. **Mono → bi → poly → integrated system.** Combine with similar or opposite systems, then
   fold the combination into a new single system (e.g. separate devices → a smartphone).
6. **Transition to the micro level.** Macro parts are replaced by smaller structures, materials,
   particles and fields that do the same job.
7. **Increasing use of fields.** Mechanical → acoustic → thermal → chemical → electrical →
   magnetic → electromagnetic and informational. In business: physical → digital → data-driven.
8. **Matching and mismatching of rhythms.** Parts become coordinated in frequency, shape and
   timing; useful deliberate mismatches avoid harm.
9. **Decreasing human involvement.** The human is gradually removed from execution, then
   control, then decision layers (manual → mechanized → automated → autonomous).
10. **Increasing segmentation of space and surfaces.** Monolithic → hollow → many cavities →
    porous → active porous (capillaries, filters, catalysts).
11. **Transition to the supersystem.** When a system's resources are exhausted, it merges into a
    larger system and becomes one of its functions (a camera becomes a phone feature).

## How to use for forecasting

1. Place the current system on its S-curve and explain why.
2. For each relevant trend, find the current stage and the next one or two stages.
3. Write concrete next-generation concepts for each "next stage".
4. Identify the contradiction that will block each step; that contradiction is where the next
   invention is needed and where opportunities are.
